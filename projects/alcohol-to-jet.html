Microchannel reactors for sustainable aviation fuel

Turning renewable ethanol into jet fuel runs through butene. I helped model the 3D‑printed microchannel reactors that make that step compact, modular, and ready to number up.

The problem

Sustainable aviation fuel can be made from ethanol, and converting ethanol to butene is a key intermediate step on that route. Microchannel reactors promise much better heat and mass transfer than conventional reactors, and additive manufacturing makes it possible to scale them by adding more identical units rather than building one giant vessel.

To design those reactors well, the team needed a model that could predict conversion and selectivity across operating conditions and geometries.

What I did

I developed a first‑principles multiphysics model in COMSOL coupling Laminar Flow, Heat Transfer in Fluids, and Transport of Diluted Species with Arrhenius‑based reaction kinetics. I compared reactor geometries across four operating conditions and validated the model against experimental data using temperature sweeps, Arrhenius trend analysis, and selectivity comparisons.

Because many kinetic parameters interact, manual tuning was slow. So I built an automated calibration loop:

  1. Solve the reactor model in COMSOL.
  2. Export conversion and selectivity to MATLAB.
  3. Score the mismatch against experimental data.
  4. Let the optimizer propose new parameters.
  5. Send them back to COMSOL and repeat until the error tolerance is met.

The result

The calibrated model matched experimental behavior across relevant operating conditions and became a design tool rather than just an analysis tool, able to predict how new geometries and conditions would perform.

The work contributes to “Translating Bench Catalysis to Modular Production via Additively Manufactured Microchannel Reactors: A 600× Numbering‑Up Case Study of Ethanol‑to‑Butene Conversion,” currently in review at Nature Energy.

What I learned

Our lab works in three design phases: design for functionality, design for manufacturing, and design for market. A trustworthy model lets you move between them quickly, and that is what makes it valuable to engineers, fabricators, and the people running the techno‑economics.
